# Yaw-rate tracking: 20261026-174911

## Answers (each from the sections below; the rule used is stated)
- Is the VESC gyro noisy? No. Parked floor 0.0012 rad/s (0.07 deg/s); moving, its 10-25 Hz band gives 0.0250 rad/s (vibration raises it 20.4x), 31 dB below the 0.86 rad/s yaw-rate signal. Even the whole gyro-LiDAR disagreement, 0.071 rad/s, is 8% of the signal, and most of it is the LiDAR's scan-matching noise (section 1's residual spectrum).
- Is /odom wrong during fast rotation? It is wrong at every rate, in two ways. It LEADS the car by 135 ms (it is v tan(delta)/L of the command, not a measurement), which alone makes its raw RMSE 0.54 rad/s; and once that lead is removed it still over-reads rotation, gain 1.12 -> 1.31 -> 1.27 over |w| bands 0.0-0.5, 0.5-1.0, 1.0-2.0 rad/s (RMSE 0.33 aligned). The over-read is the car's understeer seen from the other side, plus /odom's speed reading high. The gyro's gain in the same bands: 0.98, 1.01, 1.01.
- Is the LiDAR lagging by 60 ms? No. The gyro is -10 ms against the LiDAR (positive = gyro later), so the LiDAR is 10 ms behind the gyro; per 10 s window median -10 ms, p10..p90 -11..-8 ms over 7 windows. A 60 ms LiDAR lag would read -60 here. The ~130-160 ms delays in section 3 are the CAR's response to steering (and /odom's lead), not a sensor lag.
- Does the car consistently understeer? No; it understeers only at the grip limit. On held commands below 1.5 m/s^2 it turns 1.09x the geometry's curvature (n=130, slightly MORE than asked), and left 0.88 / right 0.85 overall match, so the steering model and calibration are right. The overall gain below 1 comes from transients (next answer), not steady understeer. Achieved / commanded lateral acceleration by commanded a_y: 1.0-1.5 1.09, 1.5-2.0 1.02, 2.0-2.5 0.92: the tyres stop delivering above ~2.0 m/s^2 (p99 achieved 2.9 m/s^2), and the rear axle slides out -0.0, -0.2, -0.7, -0.5, -1.5, -2.7 deg over achieved a_y bands 0.0-0.5, 0.5-1.0, 1.0-1.5, 1.5-2.0, 2.0-2.5, 2.5-3.0. That is limit understeer: the car pushes wide when asked for more grip than the floor has. The planners ask for up to 2.5 (src/gap_follow/config/gap_follow.yaml:130), 2.5 (src/pure_pursuit/config/pure_pursuit.yaml:129).
- Does yaw-rate tracking degrade above 3 m/s? Yes, as a continuation, not a cliff. Below 3 m/s gain 0.85, NRMSE 22%; above, gain 0.71, NRMSE 38% (17 s of data). It is not grip: only 11% of the samples above 3 m/s asked for more than the ~2.0 m/s^2 limit (p90 commanded 2.1 m/s^2). It is dynamics: the gain on a CHANGING command falls with speed, 0.99 -> 0.82 -> 0.74 -> 0.74 -> 0.74 -> 0.64 over 1.0-1.5, 1.5-2.0, 2.0-2.5, 2.5-3.0, 3.0-3.5, 3.5-4.0 m/s, and the steering lag grows from 125-135 ms below 3 m/s to 135, 175 ms above. Too few held commands above 3 m/s to measure steady tracking there directly.
- Estimator or controller? Change the estimator first; then change the controller's limits, not its gains. The gyro is 5x closer to the LiDAR than /odom (0.071 vs 0.334 rad/s, even after removing /odom's 135 ms lead), and /odom is what slam_toolbox, the particle filter and pure_pursuit consume today: gyro yaw in the odometry (vehicle_state's /odom/imu, gyro_odom:=true) improves every pose the controller sees. On the controller side the steering model is right, so do not retune it: lower max_lateral_accel to the measured ~2.0 m/s^2, and account for the ~145 ms steering lag (lookahead / the MPC's delay term). Raising steering gains would chase that lag into a weave. And slow before turning in: at the command's peak, 30 of 30 turns asked for more than the grip (median 6.7 m/s^2), because the steering reaches lock while the speed is still coming down.
