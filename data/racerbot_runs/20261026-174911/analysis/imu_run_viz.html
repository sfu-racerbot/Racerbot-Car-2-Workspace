Steering frequency response

Input: the commanded yaw rate. Output: the measured one. H(f) = Suy(f) / Suu(f), with Suy = E[U*(f)·Y(f)]. The conjugate cancels each segment's start phase, leaving ∠Y − ∠U. Points are bins with coherence ≥ 0.6. Lines are the fitted plant.

Low-frequency gain ≈ 0.8: the car turns about 20% less than the kinematic model says. A pure delay τ gives phase −360°·f·τ, a straight line in f. The phase slope gives τ ≈ 0.104 s, and the lag adds −atan(2πfT) on top. At 1 Hz: −37° − 18° ≈ −55° against −58.5° measured.

Parked gyro noise, and Parseval

Welch PSD of gyro z over the four still stretches at 50 Hz, Nyquist 25 Hz. Parseval: the variance is the area under the PSD, σ² = ∫ S(f) df. The flat floor is white noise; a peak would be vibration or an alias.

Conjugate symmetry on real data

64 samples of the gyro while turning, x[n] real, and their DFT. Because x[n] = x*[n], the conjugation property x*[n] ↔ X*[−k] forces X[−k] = X*[k]. So Re X is even and Im X is odd. Hover a bin to see its mirror. This is why rfft keeps only k ≥ 0 and one-sided spectra double every bin except DC and Nyquist.
